About Amrabad Tiger Reserve

Telangana's tiger reserve in the Nallamala hills of the Eastern Ghats.

Amrabad Tiger Reserve (ATR) lies in the Nallamala forest of Telangana, across Nagarkurnool and Nalgonda districts. It is one of the largest tiger reserves in India, covering about 2,600 sq km, with a core area of about 2,170 sq km. The river Krishna runs along its eastern edge.

The reserve was once the northern part of the Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve. It became a separate reserve after Telangana was formed in 2014. Its landscape of plateaus, deep valleys, gorges and dry deciduous forest is home to the tiger, leopard, wild dog (dhole), sloth bear, sambar, chital, chousingha, nilgai and many smaller animals. Rare species such as the rusty-spotted cat and the honey badger also live here.

The Chenchu people, a forest-dwelling tribal community, have lived in these forests for generations and take part in the reserve's protection work.
